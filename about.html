About us

A college that never lost the thread

Thirty-nine years of teaching Ayurveda in Kasoa — through three changes of regulation, one pandemic, and the arrival of a national curriculum.

Vision

Ayurveda understood on its own terms

A world in which an Ayurvedic physician is judged by the classical standards set out in the Samhitas — and by how well their patients actually do.

Mission

Teach the text and the hand

To produce practitioners who can read the primary sources, examine a patient competently, and treat them safely — with honesty about the limits of what they know.

Values

Four commitments

  • Patient welfare before institutional convenience
  • Evidence, even when it complicates tradition
  • Consent and supervision without exception
  • Access — fees published, scholarships funded
Our history

From a single room to a teaching hospital

The college began as a four-room clinic and a promise: that classical Ayurvedic education could be taught at all, and taught properly, outside the handful of institutions that already did.

  1. 1987

    The first clinic

    Divine Mystical & Ayurvedic College opens as a four-room dispensary teaching Ayurvedic medicine to fourteen students in a single room above a shop.

  2. 1994

    First full diploma cohort

    The five-and-a-half year professional diploma admits its first full cohort, and the college begins teaching as a training institution rather than a dispensary course.

  3. 2003

    Attaching a hospital

    Sixty clinical beds are opened, giving students supervised postings from their second year and making internships meaningful for the first time.

  4. 2011

    The medicinal plant garden

    Four acres of identified species are planted, supplying the pharmacy and giving pharmacognosy students a living reference collection.

  5. 2018

    Unified curriculum

    The college settles on a single curriculum across the diploma, Panchakarma and certificate programmes, so that a student can move between them without starting again.

  6. 2023

    Ethics and research

    The college's ethics committee and clinical research unit are established, with published studies from student and faculty theses.

Infrastructure

What is on site

The campus is compact and everything is within a short walk. This matters more than it sounds: a student who has to cross a city between a lecture and a ward round is a student who will skip the ward round.

  • Attached teaching hospital with 60 beds
  • Twelve-station panchakarma theatre
  • Ayurvedic pharmacy and pharmacognosy laboratory
  • Four-acre medicinal plant garden
  • Sanskrit seminar library, open to students until midnight
  • Yoga and meditation hall
  • Simulation lab for anatomy and marma points
  • Boy's and girl's hostels on campus
  • Canteen serving sattvic and Ayurveda-appropriate meals
  • Sports ground and indoor games room

Status

Where we actually stand

We would rather say this plainly than bury it. Anyone considering enrolling deserves to know it before they pay anything.

Divine Mystical & Ayurvedic College is not accredited. We are a private training college. We award our own diplomas and certificates of training, and we hold no institutional accreditation and no affiliation with any university.

That means:

· None of our awards is a university degree.
· None of them, by itself, entitles a holder to register or practise as a healthcare practitioner in Ghana or abroad.
· We do not claim recognition we cannot evidence.

We are pursuing recognition, and we will say so here the moment we have it. In the meantime, if you need a formally recognised qualification to practise, please talk to us first — we would rather talk you out of enrolling than into the wrong course.
